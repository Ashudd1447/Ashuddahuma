# Ashuddahuma

An Islamic homeschool site for a 4-year-old, built by his parents. The content comes from the parents, prompt by prompt: the father set the written curriculum outline aside on 2026-11-25 ("I will prompt you with more content"), so nothing is built from it and nothing is owed for it.

- Live site: https://ashudd.web.app
- Firebase project: `ashudd-1b0dc`, hosting site `ashudd`, web app `ashudd` (its public config is in `js/account.js`)
- GitHub: `Ashudd1447/Ashuddahuma` (public)
- Deploy: `firebase deploy --only hosting` from this folder

## Working with the parents

- Run all content past them before it goes in: photos, wording, voweling, audio. The parents give the outline and structure; content may be drafted or generated with AI tools, then a parent reviews and approves it. Nothing the child sees goes in on Claude's own initiative.
- Ask clarifying questions and consult often. Bring options with a recommendation.
- Do not commit, push, or deploy without their go-ahead.

## Content rules

- No music. Chimes for praise and a soft buzzer for errors are approved.
- Photos of people and animals may show faces. Graphics (figures, icons) must not show facial features: a plain face, with hands, clothes and other details drawn. Images may be photographs or made with AI tools (ChatGPT, Recraft) once a parent approves them. Wikimedia photos need a credit on `المصادر`; generated ones do not. The site has no photographs now: the reading game's six were replaced by Recraft pictures on 2026-11-25, at the father's word.
- A drawn animal has a plain face too: no eyes, nose or mouth. It is shown from the front or the side, never from behind: the father sent back a lion and a rabbit seen from the back (2026-11-25: "I don't like the lion and rabbit from the back. Do front or side views with no facial features"). And things keep their true size to each other across pictures that stand side by side: "the rabbit can't look fatter/bigger than the lion", so the lion fills his square and the rabbit is small in hers.
- In every picture, people eat, drink and brush their teeth with the right hand, and nobody sleeps on the tummy (on the right side, the blanket up to the shoulders and over the feet). Check generated pictures for this as for faces: image models get hands wrong often.
- Generated pictures come only from the family's paid Recraft plan (Basic, from 2026-11-19). Free-plan images are public and owned by Recraft, and upgrading does not transfer them, so a Free-plan picture never goes on the site. How to make them: `docs/recraft-images.md`.
- Pictures of wudu and the prayer follow the father's corrections of 2026-11-25. In sujud the face is to the ground. Standing, he is at the end of his rug, facing along it, eyes on the place of sujud, never facing the viewer. In qiyam the right hand lies over the left, covering it. Sitting is iftirash: on the left foot laid flat, the right foot upright on bent toes that point forward, joined to its leg. The right index finger points in the tashahhud. Rising from the bowing, the hands are raised beside the ears as in the opening takbir. The salam turns the head to the right, then to the left, the body straight and the hands on the thighs. In wudu the right hand opens the tap and takes the water, right comes before left, the sleeves are above the elbows, the head is wiped with the right hand alone, nothing is wet before its turn, and no finger is raised afterward. The hero is the boy of the family's printed books «مُوسَى الطَّيَّارُ»: black hair with a tuft, navy sweater, red scarf, dark trousers, plain face, the same in every picture. Check each such picture at full size before keeping it: hands and feet counted, right and left worked out (the image model takes the far arm for every action, and draws third hands). How they were made: `docs/recraft-images.md`.
- No churches or other non-Islamic religious buildings in photos. Check the source file name and page, not just the picture.
- Everything the child sees is in Arabic, with no English. Two exceptions: the `المصادر` page, where names and license codes may stay in Latin letters, and the admin pages, which are in English. English may be heard, never read, in one place: the 1 2 3 batch of `صِلْ كُلَّ رَقْمٍ بِالصُّورَةِ الْمُنَاسِبَةِ لَهُ` says the numbers' English names, which the father records (his request, 2026-11-25).
- Modern Standard Arabic spelling everywhere except Quran text: the circular sukoon, no dagger alif, and so on. Words the child reads are fully voweled, buttons and menu labels included (`اِسْتَمِعْ`, `أَعِدِ الْآيَةَ`, `الْآيَةُ التَّالِيَةُ`, `فُصْحَى`).
- Exceptions in the reading game: `أحسنت` is displayed with no vowel marks and recorded with a sukoon on the ت, so it is gender neutral. The prompts `اقرأ` and `اسحب كل صورة إلى كلمتها` and the title `معلم القراءة` appear exactly as the parents wrote them.
- Two dialects. A spoken prompt may exist in Fusha (`audio/<name>`) and Egyptian (`audio/<name>-masri`); the dialect switch in the ☰ menu (showing `فُصْحَى` or the Egyptian flag, and saying `فصحى` / `مصري` when tapped) picks which one plays, and `sound.say(name)` falls back to Fusha when the Egyptian clip is missing. Counting and every other spoken prompt follow this rule unless the parents specify one dialect.
- The words on the screen are the words the voice says. The father, 2026-11-25: "The text can't read أمام while the voice says قدام." A first answer hid the Fusha text, with speaker marks in place of what was tapped; on 2026-11-26 he found a page of speaker marks could not be told apart ("You can't tell what each speaker does") and changed the rule: when the Egyptian recording of a text is the one that plays, the Egyptian text shows in its place. `sound.masri(name)` answers whether the Egyptian recording will play (the switch on مصري and `<name>-masri` recorded), and the window event `dialectchange` follows the switch. While a text's Egyptian clip is not recorded, the Fusha recording plays and the Fusha text shows. The Egyptian text is exactly the recording card's (`record.js`, and `where-words.js` for `أَيْنَ؟`), which `tools/list-egyptian.js` writes to `public/egyptian.json` (run by `prepare-deploy.py`), so the parents record each line as the card reads, or change the card first. It is shown as written there, with few vowel marks. `أَيْنَ؟` has its own code (its step texts in the dialect each one's recording is heard in); every other text goes through `sound.caption(el, text, clip)` in `sound.js`, which shows the Fusha or the Egyptian, follows the switch, and keeps the text unseen on Egyptian until it is known which plays. Every new game uses `sound.caption` for every text a recording says. `docs/egyptian-text-rule.md` lists the texts.
- The salah door is Fusha only, text and voice (the father, 2026-11-26: "Remove Egyptian text/recording from the Salah activity altogether. This includes wudu, salah, prayer times, etc."): it has no `-masri` cards, `salah.js` and `prayers.js` never look for `-masri` (the qibla's `place-masjid`, shared with the driving games, stays Fusha there), and its pages load their clips with `sound.preload`, not `preloadPrompts`, so no dialect switch shows on them.
- Where an activity is recorded in both dialects, the switch stands on the page itself, so the parent sees there is a choice (the father, 2026-11-25: "there should be something to signal that you should toggle"; he chose this over a pop-up). A small two-sided switch, `فُصْحَى` and the flag with the chosen side filled, stands beside the ✕ and ☰ (on a phone in the top left corner, where the row has room). It is there from the start of the activity when anything in the whole activity has both recordings, breathes twice as the page opens, on every visit, and then rests: never again for each word ("the user already knows"). A tap switches and says the dialect's name. An activity with no Egyptian recording shows no switch; the one in the ☰ menu stays everywhere. Since the first big sitting (2026-11-26) it shows in `أَيْنَ؟`, the number matching game, the reciter, the clock and the books' counting games, and since the second (2026-11-27) in the color board and `اِجْمَعْ وَعُدَّ`; on a phone a star counter in that corner (the clock's, the driving games', the building game's, the car factory's) steps down under it.
- The installed app is named `أَشُدَّهُمَا`. The home page tab title stays `معلم القراءة`.
- Quran text is copied from a verified source, never typed by hand, and never altered. Ayah 18:82 comes from the `text_uthmani` field of the Quran.com API, with the word `أشدهما` in gold. Surah titles are the one piece of Quranic-style text typed by hand, in Uthmani spelling and the Mushaf font: `سُورَةُ ٱلْفَاتِحَةِ`.
- The Mushaf font draws the khaa-head sukoon on U+06E1 and the silent-letter circle on U+0652, while the Quran.com text writes the sukoon as U+0652; its digits already sit inside the ayah ornament, so U+06DD before a number draws a second, empty one. `mushafText()` in `app.js` swaps and drops these for display only. The stored text and the HTML keep the API's characters. Ayah numbers in the Mushaf font are the bare Arabic-Indic digits.
- Hadith and du'a text is copied from a verified source by a script, never typed, and checked by the parents: `tools/make-riding-dua.py` writes `dua/riding.json` from `docs/sources/abudawud-2602.json` (Sunan Abi Dawud 2602 as printed at sunnah.com) and, for the verses inside it, the Quran.com API. The one change the script makes is spelling: sunnah.com stores the marks of lam-alif after the alif, and they are moved onto the lam.
- Signing in is optional and says so (`اِخْتِيَارِيٌّ`); the whole site works signed out. For now an account only identifies the admin. Child profiles under a parent will build on it later; what signing up gives the family is to be written then.

## Code

Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript in `public/`, with no build step and nothing to install.

- `index.html`: the optional sign-in button (top left), logo, ayah 18:82, then the doors: `اللُّغَةُ الْعَرَبِيَّةُ` (`arabic.html`, scattered letters drawn in SVG in Noto Naskh, أ ض ع ت د among them, where the reading game's card used to stand), the Fatihah card, `الصَّلَاةُ` (`salah.html`), `الْأَرْقَامُ وَالْحِسَابُ` (`numbers.html`), `الْمُخْتَبَرُ` (`lab.html`, a microscope) and `الرِّيَاضَةُ` (`sports.html`, the archer), each a hub of its own with its picture in `images/doors/`, `images/salah/` or `images/sports/` (the father, 2026-11-25: these four stand on the home page, not inside the games); the `أَلْعَاب` door (the masjid being built, the building game's own drawing), the `الْقَصَصُ` door (a book standing on a stack of three plain books, drawn in SVG, its cover the first story's picture and title), the install button, and at the very foot, under `المصادر`, the version this device runs as a date and hour in the device's time zone (`الْإِصْدَارُ: ٢٤ نُوفَمْبِر ٢٠٢٦، ٩:٢٦`, the father's choice over a number: `CACHE` in `sw.js` is the deploy's UTC time, asked of the service worker, read from `sw.js` before one answers), so the family can tell which version they have
- `games.html`: `أَلْعَاب`, the games hub. One card per finished game, no placeholders for games still to come. Inside a game the logo leads back here, as it leads home from the hub and the lessons. Two cards are doors to hubs of their own: `making.html` (`الْبِنَاءُ وَالتَّصْنِيعُ`), which holds the building game and the car factory, and `think.html` (`فَكِّرْ`, two games of its own, then the books' thinking games); a game's logo and ✕ lead back to its door, and the door's lead here. The design plan for the games, with the parents' decisions, is `docs/games-plan.md`; how to make their pictures in Recraft is `docs/recraft-images.md`.
- `blocks.html`, `js/blocks.js`: `الْمُكَعَّبَاتُ`, the block puzzle. A 6×6 board and three pieces at a time (none longer than four), dragged on with large targets; on a touch screen the piece rides above the finger, and it drops on the nearest spot where it fits. A full row or column bursts in a wave, one star per line flies to the counter (top left, Arabic-Indic digits), and the praise shows and plays: `أحسنت` (`well-done`) for one line, `مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ` (`mashaallah`) for two or more or a cleared board. No losing: a piece with no room is dimmed, and when none has room the `قِطَعٌ جَدِيدَةٌ` button deals three that fit. Every deal fits the board, and often includes a piece that would finish a line. The board is kept in `localStorage`; `لَوْحَةٌ جَدِيدَةٌ` in the ☰ menu starts over. Tiles are glazed colors with a diamond inlay (`[data-tile]` in `style.css`); wide screens put the tray in a column beside the board.
- `clock.html`, `js/clock.js`: `السَّاعَةُ`, telling the time (whole hours for now, no prayer times: the prayers get their own game). One big clock whose hands the child turns; they move together as on a real clock. A touch takes the nearer hand (near the middle, the short one). Let go and the clock springs to the nearest whole hour and says it (`hour-1` to `hour-12`, in the chosen dialect); a tap on the clock says it again. The card above shows an hour and says it when tapped (in Egyptian when the Egyptian recording is the one that plays, by the father's rule); when the clock settles on it, a chime, a star to the counter, `أحسنت`, and a new hour. Any other hour is just said, never buzzed. The sky behind follows the time of day (sun right to left, moon and stars at night, the town's windows lit), and the clock opens at the real time, rounded to the hour. Two round buttons pick the way to play, remembered with the stars in `localStorage`: the hours (a clock icon), or "my day" (the boy's picture), where the card shows a picture of his day (`images/day/<name>.webp`, from Recraft Basic) with its hour, in the order of the day, starting from the next one after the time now; when the clock matches, the sky turns to that time of day. The scene hours in `DAY` in `clock.js` are placeholders until the parents give the real ones.
- `mazes.html`, `js/mazes.js`: `الْمَتَاهَاتُ`, the mazes: fifty, used as printed: the family's first seven and the forty-three of the two printed books «مُوسَى الطَّيَّارُ» that were not among them (2026-11-24; seven book pages were the first seven again). `mazes/mazes.json` is written by `tools/make-mazes.py`, which reads the walls out of the pages' own drawing (`docs/sources/arabic-mazes.pdf` with the standard library; the books, kept outside the repo in `../books/musa-go.pdf` and `musa-back.pdf`, with PyMuPDF; without the books it keeps what the file already holds), so no wall is retyped; never edit it by hand. It proves every maze has exactly one way through. A maze's number is kept in the saved marks: 1 to 7, then 100 plus its page in the first book, 200 plus its page in the second. The list has three sections: `مَتَاهَاتٌ` (smallest first), `سَاعِدْ` (five mazes with a picture at each end, `images/mazes/*.png` cut from the books: a car and a house, a plane and a cloud, a bus and a masjid, a bike and a tree, a balloon and the sun, each with its own instruction, `maze-help-*`), and `مَتَاهَاتٌ فِيهَا رَسْمٌ مَخْفِيٌّ` (twelve). The new hidden pictures are a house, a tree, an airplane from above, a sailboat, a car from the side, an umbrella and a mug: Claude's and a reviewer's readings, for the parents to confirm. A list of cards (a finished maze shows its way through and a star, or its hidden picture and name; kept in `localStorage` as `{v: 1, done}`; no star counter), then the maze on a canvas with the printed page's own marks: a faint green disc and an arrow pointing in with `اِبْدَأْ هُنَا`, a faint red disc and an arrow pointing out with `النِّهَايَةُ`, and the page's instruction above, shown and said (`maze-draw` for the first two, `maze-find` for the rest). The child draws his own line with a finger: it follows where the finger really went, never touches a wall, rewinds when he goes back the way he came, and waits at its head when the finger crosses a wall (a finger that slips into a cell at most `FAR` passages away is walked round to, but the end of a dead end is entered only by the finger itself, through its opening). A touch takes the line up only near its head, which a ring marks. He may make mistakes: every corridor is open. At the end of a dead end (the finger 0.3 of a cell in: `MIDDLE`) there is a soft buzz, a vibration where the device has one, the line turns red and fades, and he starts over: the father's rule, and the one place on the site where something is lost. At the exit, a chime. Mazes 1 and 2 then show `أحسنت`. In mazes 3 to 7 the way through draws a hidden picture: the walls fade, `مَاذَا رَسَمْتَ؟` shows and is said (`maze-what`), the child's uneven line morphs into the picture's clean outline (each of his points moving to its own place on it, through `picture.anchors`), and the picture's name shows and is said: `مُثَلَّجَات`, `تُفَّاحَة`, `فَانُوس`, `هِلَال`, `مَسْجِد` (the last with the reading game's `mosque` recording). The mazes were made in another chat, so the names are Claude's reading of the pictures: the parents confirm them, the third and the fifth above all. The outlines and the names are written by hand in `tools/make-mazes.py` (`PICTURES`). On localhost, `window.mazeGame` steps it by hand (`open`, `point`, `key`, `solve`, `update`, `draw`).
- `typing.html`, `js/typing.js`, `js/typing-engine.js`: `الطِّبَاعَةُ`, touch typing for Arabic, for a child and for a grown-up. The father asked for what keybr.com does for English, researched for Arabic; he was asleep while it was built, so all of it is Claude's choice until he has tried it. One page and one engine: `typing-engine.js` decides everything and has no page in it (`node tools/test-typing-engine.js` plays whole learners through it), and `typing.js` draws, listens and saves. The first visit asks who plays (`طِفْل` or `كَبِير`); the two are kept apart, in `localStorage` under `typing`. Three ways to play, chosen by the page and changeable in the settings (the gear at the top left; everything in it is Arabic): real keys (a computer, or a tablet with a keyboard: the drawn keyboard is the guide, with the next key lit, the Shift of the other hand lit when needed, and the fingers' zones tinted); tapping the drawn keyboard (a touch screen at least 760 by 500 CSS pixels, 1000 wide for the child); and the small version below that (the target's key with its real neighbors as six large keys, a small map of the keyboard, and a strip that says `النُّسْخَةُ الصَّغِيرَةُ`). The keyboards are data, `typing/layouts.json`, written by `tools/make-typing-layouts.py` from Unicode CLDR 43 and XKB and checked by `tools/check-typing-layouts.py` (never edit it by hand): Windows Arabic (102), the default because it is what this family's computer has, Arabic (101), which differs from it in four characters, (102) AZERTY, the Mac's Arabic and its PC-ordered Arabic, and Android's touch keyboard. A key press is read by its place (`event.code` with Shift, through the chosen keyboard) and not by `event.key`, which Chrome on Windows gets wrong for the lam-alef key and some Shift keys, and which is Latin when the system is on English; `event.key` is only evidence, and after two presses that fit another keyboard the game changes to it and says so. A keyboard not yet played starts from the record of the one played most, keeping every character whose key is the same on both. The order in which letters are taught is worked out for each keyboard: a letter's share in real text (`typing/stats.json`) divided by the effort of its key (row, finger, reach, Shift); the first six by that alone, then, among the three highest, the letter that opens the most common words; no Shift letter before twelve are in play. On the Arabic PC keyboards the home row holds seven of the nine commonest letters, so the course starts `ا ل ي ن م ت`. A grown-up starts with six letters and types real words only (`typing/words.json`: 3,840 words found in three corpora, read twice and filtered by `docs/sources/typing/blocked-words.txt`, written by `tools/make-typing-data.py`, whose raw sources stay in a git-ignored cache); a mistake stops the line; a new letter comes when every letter in play has been mastered (speed against the target, first-try accuracy and enough samples, `K = S × A × N`), or at the latest after ten rounds without one, so nobody is stuck; when all are known, the weak common pairs become the focus. A child gets one letter at a time on a large card with its key lit, ten to a round, two new letters a day at most, no numbers and no star counter: the strip of letters is his progress. The lam-alef keys are taught as keys of their own, and in the line of words a lam and the alef after it stay one element (`pieces()`), or the لا ligature falls apart into a lam and an alef. The marks are a switch for the grown-up on real keys (voweled words, the marks coming in one at a time, so words show partly voweled meanwhile). Digits and punctuation are not in yet. The constants that will need tuning sit at the top of the engine (`CONSTANTS`). The family's Galaxy Tab showed the child a blank page that no emulated screen reproduced, so the page guards itself: if a round cannot begin, or the drawn keyboard is not on the screen 0.8 s after it begins, it falls back to the small version for that visit (on glass only), and if even that fails the player starts afresh with the old record kept under `typing-backup`; every error, with the screen size, the way and the browser, is kept in `localStorage` under `typing-errors` (the last five), which the admin page lists. On localhost, `window.typingGame` (`game()` is the engine's game).
- `drive.html`, `js/drive.js`: `قِيَادَةُ الْمَرْكَبَاتِ`, the driving games. Pick a road (`الْحَيُّ`, to the masjid; `كُورْنِيشُ النِّيلِ`, to the grandmother's house), drag the seat belt's tongue to the buckle (`اِرْبِطْ حِزَامَ الْأَمَانِ`, spoken as `belt`; the seat is empty, no figure), see the du'a of riding (`dua/riding.json`: `بِسْمِ اللَّهِ`, then the verses, and nothing else, the parents' choice; its words light from the `riding-dua` recording through `timing.autoWords`, and the go button comes when it ends, or after a moment while there is no recording), and drive. The ✕ at the top leaves a drive for the list of roads. Each drive has a light background sound made in `sound.js` (`ambience()`): birds in the neighborhood; wind over the water, and birds, on the corniche (the parents heard that wind as waves, and kept it for this road only). The road is drawn on a canvas straight from above and scrolls at one size: an earlier tilted view, where things grew as they came nearer, made the parents dizzy. Lengths are in lanes, the road three lanes wide. Cars are drawn in code; buildings, trees, boats and people are the Recraft drawings in `images/drive/*.svg`, turned into bitmaps once per screen size. Lamps and palms stand on the walk; everything else stands in a row beyond it, nothing in the road and nothing in front of anything else. Steering is by turning the tablet like a wheel and is absolute ("straight is straight", never set at the start): `steerAngle()` takes which way is down from `deviceorientation`, turns it by `window.orientation` (not `screen.orientation.angle` first: on iPad that counts from landscape), and when the system rotates the picture mid-turn keeps the old way up until the tablet is level the new way; 3° dead zone, full lock at 26°. iPhone and iPad show their Allow box on each launch, asked from the seat belt's release and again from the go button. With no sensor, or when a parent picks finger steering in the ☰ menu, a finger on the road steers; arrow keys always work. Two pedals, either one: held, the car speeds up to 12 lanes a second (15 on the roads seen from behind: `SPEED`); let go, it slows. Steering is quick (`STEER.rate`: a lane in about a sixth of a second at full lock): the father wants a little rush. A light turns amber far enough ahead for that speed. Traffic is laid out in rows well apart with a lane always open, and all of it keeps one speed, so there is always a way past; after a stop it pulls away lane by lane. No losing: the car follows traffic by itself, a touch is a soft bump with a horn, and it stops itself at a red light or for people crossing (the `stops` of a road in `ROADS`), the pedals showing a red hand meanwhile. It brakes for a stop whether or not a pedal is held, and slows for an amber light as for a red one: when the roads were made faster (12 and 15 lanes a second) a car coasting with the pedal let go, or one that sped up during the amber, ran the light, which the father reported on 2026-11-24. People wait at the curb for a car already on the crossing. `node tools/test-drive-stops.js` drives the four roads 252 times against the local site and fails if a light or a crossing is passed; a stop belongs at least 30 lanes before a road's end. The reward is on the road: stars in short lines to drive over, taken without a sound (a ping for every star was too much for the father), and five more for arriving; stopping at a light earns nothing. A line of stars is never behind slow traffic: it takes its lane only as it comes into view (the lane with the fewest cars ahead, `placeLines()`), and a car in that lane signals and moves over, or holds back until it can (`yieldToStars()`). From 50 lanes before the end the right turn signal blinks (amber lamps on the car, two arrowheads beside it, a soft tick from `sound.blink()`), so the child knows the stop is near. Then the car takes itself to the right lane, turns into a side street and stops at the door of the goal while the picture slides over to follow. The place's name shows and is said (`الْمَسْجِدُ` / `place-masjid`, `بَيْتُ جَدَّتِي` / `place-grandma`), then `الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ` (`alhamdulillah`); at the masjid the first words of the adhan play (`audio/adhan-1.mp3`, the one of three free recordings the parents kept, cut by `tools/cut-mp3.py` and credited on `المصادر`; with more files and a higher `ADHANS` they take turns); at her house the grandmother comes out and waves. Roads are data in `ROADS`. Two of them are there twice, for the parents to compare: `الْحَيُّ الْجَدِيدُ` and `الْكُورْنِيشُ الْجَدِيدُ` (`chase: true`) are the same drives seen from behind the car (`js/chase.js`), and the pair the parents do not keep is to be deleted. Stars, the steering choice and the adhan's turn are kept in `localStorage`. The tablet's rotation lock should be on while playing, or a big turn of the wheel rotates the picture.
