# Ashuddahuma

An Islamic homeschool site for a 4-year-old, built by his parents. The curriculum is in `Ashuddahuma Homeschool — Curriculum Outline (Age 4) V2.pdf`, one folder above this repo.

- Live site: https://ashudd.web.app
- Firebase project: `ashudd-1b0dc`, hosting site `ashudd`, web app `ashudd` (its public config is in `js/account.js`)
- GitHub: `Ashudd1447/Ashuddahuma` (public)
- Deploy: `firebase deploy --only hosting` from this folder

## Working with the parents

- Run all content past them before it goes in: photos, wording, voweling, audio. The parents give the outline and structure; content may be drafted or generated with AI tools, then a parent reviews and approves it. Nothing the child sees goes in on Claude's own initiative.
- Ask clarifying questions and consult often. Bring options with a recommendation.
- Do not commit, push, or deploy without their go-ahead.

## Content rules

- No music. Chimes for praise and a soft buzzer for errors are approved.
- Photos of people and animals may show faces. Graphics (figures, icons) must not show facial features: a plain face, with hands, clothes and other details drawn. Images may be photographs or made with AI tools (ChatGPT, Recraft) once a parent approves them. Wikimedia photos need a credit on `المصادر`; generated ones do not.
- In every picture, people eat, drink and brush their teeth with the right hand, and nobody sleeps on the tummy (on the right side, the blanket up to the shoulders and over the feet). Check generated pictures for this as for faces: image models get hands wrong often.
- Generated pictures come only from the family's paid Recraft plan (Basic, from 2026-11-19). Free-plan images are public and owned by Recraft, and upgrading does not transfer them, so a Free-plan picture never goes on the site. How to make them: `docs/recraft-images.md`.
- No churches or other non-Islamic religious buildings in photos. Check the source file name and page, not just the picture.
- Everything the child sees is in Arabic, with no English. Two exceptions: the `المصادر` page, where names and license codes may stay in Latin letters, and the admin pages, which are in English.
- Modern Standard Arabic spelling everywhere except Quran text: the circular sukoon, no dagger alif, and so on. Words the child reads are fully voweled, buttons and menu labels included (`اِسْتَمِعْ`, `أَعِدِ الْآيَةَ`, `الْآيَةُ التَّالِيَةُ`, `فُصْحَى`).
- Exceptions in the reading game: `أحسنت` is displayed with no vowel marks and recorded with a sukoon on the ت, so it is gender neutral. The prompts `اقرأ` and `اسحب كل صورة إلى كلمتها` and the title `معلم القراءة` appear exactly as the parents wrote them.
- Two dialects, one text. On-screen text is always Fusha. A spoken prompt may exist in Fusha (`audio/<name>`) and Egyptian (`audio/<name>-masri`); the dialect switch in the ☰ menu (showing `فُصْحَى` or the Egyptian flag, and saying `فصحى` / `مصري` when tapped) picks which one plays, and `sound.say(name)` falls back to Fusha when the Egyptian clip is missing. Egyptian text appears only in `record.html`, to be read aloud. Counting and every other spoken prompt follow this rule unless the parents specify one dialect.
- The installed app is named `أَشُدَّهُمَا`. The home page tab title stays `معلم القراءة`.
- Quran text is copied from a verified source, never typed by hand, and never altered. Ayah 18:82 comes from the `text_uthmani` field of the Quran.com API, with the word `أشدهما` in gold. Surah titles are the one piece of Quranic-style text typed by hand, in Uthmani spelling and the Mushaf font: `سُورَةُ ٱلْفَاتِحَةِ`.
- The Mushaf font draws the khaa-head sukoon on U+06E1 and the silent-letter circle on U+0652, while the Quran.com text writes the sukoon as U+0652; its digits already sit inside the ayah ornament, so U+06DD before a number draws a second, empty one. `mushafText()` in `app.js` swaps and drops these for display only. The stored text and the HTML keep the API's characters. Ayah numbers in the Mushaf font are the bare Arabic-Indic digits.
- Hadith and du'a text is copied from a verified source by a script, never typed, and checked by the parents: `tools/make-riding-dua.py` writes `dua/riding.json` from `docs/sources/abudawud-2602.json` (Sunan Abi Dawud 2602 as printed at sunnah.com) and, for the verses inside it, the Quran.com API. The one change the script makes is spelling: sunnah.com stores the marks of lam-alif after the alif, and they are moved onto the lam.
- Signing in is optional and says so (`اِخْتِيَارِيٌّ`); the whole site works signed out. For now an account only identifies the admin. Child profiles under a parent will build on it later; what signing up gives the family is to be written then.

## Code

Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript in `public/`, with no build step and nothing to install.

- `index.html`: the optional sign-in button (top left), logo, ayah 18:82, a card for each lesson, the `أَلْعَاب` door (an arch of colored tiles, drawn in SVG), the `الْقَصَصُ` door (a book standing on a stack of three plain books, drawn in SVG, its cover the first story's picture and title), and the install button
- `games.html`: `أَلْعَاب`, the games hub. One card per finished game, no placeholders for games still to come. Inside a game the logo leads back here, as it leads home from the hub and the lessons. One card is a door to a hub of its own, `making.html` (`الْبِنَاءُ وَالتَّصْنِيعُ`), which holds the building game and the car factory, one card each: their logo and ✕ lead back to that door, and the door's lead here. The design plan for the games, with the parents' decisions, is `docs/games-plan.md`; how to make their pictures in Recraft is `docs/recraft-images.md`.
- `blocks.html`, `js/blocks.js`: `الْمُكَعَّبَاتُ`, the block puzzle. A 6×6 board and three pieces at a time (none longer than four), dragged on with large targets; on a touch screen the piece rides above the finger, and it drops on the nearest spot where it fits. A full row or column bursts in a wave, one star per line flies to the counter (top left, Arabic-Indic digits), and the praise shows and plays: `أحسنت` (`well-done`) for one line, `مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ` (`mashaallah`) for two or more or a cleared board. No losing: a piece with no room is dimmed, and when none has room the `قِطَعٌ جَدِيدَةٌ` button deals three that fit. Every deal fits the board, and often includes a piece that would finish a line. The board is kept in `localStorage`; `لَوْحَةٌ جَدِيدَةٌ` in the ☰ menu starts over. Tiles are glazed colors with a diamond inlay (`[data-tile]` in `style.css`); wide screens put the tray in a column beside the board.
- `clock.html`, `js/clock.js`: `السَّاعَةُ`, telling the time (whole hours for now, no prayer times: the prayers get their own game). One big clock whose hands the child turns; they move together as on a real clock. A touch takes the nearer hand (near the middle, the short one). Let go and the clock springs to the nearest whole hour and says it (`hour-1` to `hour-12`, in the chosen dialect); a tap on the clock says it again. The card above shows an hour and says it when tapped; when the clock settles on it, a chime, a star to the counter, `أحسنت`, and a new hour. Any other hour is just said, never buzzed. The sky behind follows the time of day (sun right to left, moon and stars at night, the town's windows lit), and the clock opens at the real time, rounded to the hour. Two round buttons pick the way to play, remembered with the stars in `localStorage`: the hours (a clock icon), or "my day" (the boy's picture), where the card shows a picture of his day (`images/day/<name>.webp`, from Recraft Basic) with its hour, in the order of the day, starting from the next one after the time now; when the clock matches, the sky turns to that time of day. The scene hours in `DAY` in `clock.js` are placeholders until the parents give the real ones.
- `mazes.html`, `js/mazes.js`: `الْمَتَاهَاتُ`, the mazes: the family's seven printed mazes, used as printed. `mazes/mazes.json` is written by `tools/make-mazes.py`, which reads the walls out of the worksheet's own drawing (`docs/sources/arabic-mazes.pdf`), so no wall is retyped; never edit it by hand. A list of seven cards (a finished maze shows its way through and a star, or its hidden picture and name; kept in `localStorage` as `{v: 1, done}`; no star counter), then the maze on a canvas with the printed page's own marks: a faint green disc and an arrow pointing in with `اِبْدَأْ هُنَا`, a faint red disc and an arrow pointing out with `النِّهَايَةُ`, and the page's instruction above, shown and said (`maze-draw` for the first two, `maze-find` for the rest). The child draws his own line with a finger: it follows where the finger really went, never touches a wall, rewinds when he goes back the way he came, and waits at its head when the finger crosses a wall (a finger that slips into a cell at most `FAR` passages away is walked round to, but the end of a dead end is entered only by the finger itself, through its opening). A touch takes the line up only near its head, which a ring marks. He may make mistakes: every corridor is open. At the end of a dead end (the finger 0.3 of a cell in: `MIDDLE`) there is a soft buzz, a vibration where the device has one, the line turns red and fades, and he starts over: the father's rule, and the one place on the site where something is lost. At the exit, a chime. Mazes 1 and 2 then show `أحسنت`. In mazes 3 to 7 the way through draws a hidden picture: the walls fade, `مَاذَا رَسَمْتَ؟` shows and is said (`maze-what`), the child's uneven line morphs into the picture's clean outline (each of his points moving to its own place on it, through `picture.anchors`), and the picture's name shows and is said: `مُثَلَّجَات`, `تُفَّاحَة`, `فَانُوس`, `هِلَال`, `مَسْجِد` (the last with the reading game's `mosque` recording). The mazes were made in another chat, so the names are Claude's reading of the pictures: the parents confirm them, the third and the fifth above all. The outlines and the names are written by hand in `tools/make-mazes.py` (`PICTURES`). On localhost, `window.mazeGame` steps it by hand (`open`, `point`, `key`, `solve`, `update`, `draw`).
- `typing.html`, `js/typing.js`, `js/typing-engine.js`: `الطِّبَاعَةُ`, touch typing for Arabic, for a child and for a grown-up. The father asked for what keybr.com does for English, researched for Arabic; he was asleep while it was built, so all of it is Claude's choice until he has tried it. One page and one engine: `typing-engine.js` decides everything and has no page in it (`node tools/test-typing-engine.js` plays whole learners through it), and `typing.js` draws, listens and saves. The first visit asks who plays (`طِفْل` or `كَبِير`); the two are kept apart, in `localStorage` under `typing`. Three ways to play, chosen by the page and changeable in the settings (the gear at the top left; everything in it is Arabic): real keys (a computer, or a tablet with a keyboard: the drawn keyboard is the guide, with the next key lit, the Shift of the other hand lit when needed, and the fingers' zones tinted); tapping the drawn keyboard (a touch screen at least 760 by 500 CSS pixels, 1000 wide for the child); and the small version below that (the target's key with its real neighbors as six large keys, a small map of the keyboard, and a strip that says `النُّسْخَةُ الصَّغِيرَةُ`). The keyboards are data, `typing/layouts.json`, written by `tools/make-typing-layouts.py` from Unicode CLDR 43 and XKB and checked by `tools/check-typing-layouts.py` (never edit it by hand): Windows Arabic (102), the default because it is what this family's computer has, Arabic (101), which differs from it in four characters, (102) AZERTY, the Mac's Arabic and its PC-ordered Arabic, and Android's touch keyboard. A key press is read by its place (`event.code` with Shift, through the chosen keyboard) and not by `event.key`, which Chrome on Windows gets wrong for the lam-alef key and some Shift keys, and which is Latin when the system is on English; `event.key` is only evidence, and after two presses that fit another keyboard the game changes to it and says so. A keyboard not yet played starts from the record of the one played most, keeping every character whose key is the same on both. The order in which letters are taught is worked out for each keyboard: a letter's share in real text (`typing/stats.json`) divided by the effort of its key (row, finger, reach, Shift); the first six by that alone, then, among the three highest, the letter that opens the most common words; no Shift letter before twelve are in play. On the Arabic PC keyboards the home row holds seven of the nine commonest letters, so the course starts `ا ل ي ن م ت`. A grown-up starts with six letters and types real words only (`typing/words.json`: 3,840 words found in three corpora, read twice and filtered by `docs/sources/typing/blocked-words.txt`, written by `tools/make-typing-data.py`, whose raw sources stay in a git-ignored cache); a mistake stops the line; a new letter comes when every letter in play has been mastered (speed against the target, first-try accuracy and enough samples, `K = S × A × N`), or at the latest after ten rounds without one, so nobody is stuck; when all are known, the weak common pairs become the focus. A child gets one letter at a time on a large card with its key lit, ten to a round, two new letters a day at most, no numbers and no star counter: the strip of letters is his progress. The lam-alef keys are taught as keys of their own, and in the line of words a lam and the alef after it stay one element (`pieces()`), or the لا ligature falls apart into a lam and an alef. The marks are a switch for the grown-up on real keys (voweled words, the marks coming in one at a time, so words show partly voweled meanwhile). Digits and punctuation are not in yet. The constants that will need tuning sit at the top of the engine (`CONSTANTS`). The family's Galaxy Tab showed the child a blank page that no emulated screen reproduced, so the page guards itself: if a round cannot begin, or the drawn keyboard is not on the screen 0.8 s after it begins, it falls back to the small version for that visit (on glass only), and if even that fails the player starts afresh with the old record kept under `typing-backup`; every error, with the screen size, the way and the browser, is kept in `localStorage` under `typing-errors` (the last five), which the admin page lists. On localhost, `window.typingGame` (`game()` is the engine's game).
- `drive.html`, `js/drive.js`: `قِيَادَةُ الْمَرْكَبَاتِ`, the driving games. Pick a road (`الْحَيُّ`, to the masjid; `كُورْنِيشُ النِّيلِ`, to the grandmother's house), drag the seat belt's tongue to the buckle (`اِرْبِطْ حِزَامَ الْأَمَانِ`, spoken as `belt`; the seat is empty, no figure), see the du'a of riding (`dua/riding.json`: `بِسْمِ اللَّهِ`, then the verses, and nothing else, the parents' choice; its words light from the `riding-dua` recording through `timing.autoWords`, and the go button comes when it ends, or after a moment while there is no recording), and drive. The ✕ at the top leaves a drive for the list of roads. Each drive has a light background sound made in `sound.js` (`ambience()`): birds in the neighborhood; wind over the water, and birds, on the corniche (the parents heard that wind as waves, and kept it for this road only). The road is drawn on a canvas straight from above and scrolls at one size: an earlier tilted view, where things grew as they came nearer, made the parents dizzy. Lengths are in lanes, the road three lanes wide. Cars are drawn in code; buildings, trees, boats and people are the Recraft drawings in `images/drive/*.svg`, turned into bitmaps once per screen size. Lamps and palms stand on the walk; everything else stands in a row beyond it, nothing in the road and nothing in front of anything else. Steering is by turning the tablet like a wheel and is absolute ("straight is straight", never set at the start): `steerAngle()` takes which way is down from `deviceorientation`, turns it by `window.orientation` (not `screen.orientation.angle` first: on iPad that counts from landscape), and when the system rotates the picture mid-turn keeps the old way up until the tablet is level the new way; 3° dead zone, full lock at 26°. iPhone and iPad show their Allow box on each launch, asked from the seat belt's release and again from the go button. With no sensor, or when a parent picks finger steering in the ☰ menu, a finger on the road steers; arrow keys always work. Two pedals, either one: held, the car speeds up to 12 lanes a second (15 on the roads seen from behind: `SPEED`); let go, it slows. Steering is quick (`STEER.rate`: a lane in about a sixth of a second at full lock): the father wants a little rush. A light turns amber far enough ahead for that speed. Traffic is laid out in rows well apart with a lane always open, and all of it keeps one speed, so there is always a way past; after a stop it pulls away lane by lane. No losing: the car follows traffic by itself, a touch is a soft bump with a horn, and it stops itself at a red light or for people crossing (the `stops` of a road in `ROADS`), the pedals showing a red hand meanwhile. The reward is on the road: stars in short lines to drive over, taken without a sound (a ping for every star was too much for the father), and five more for arriving; stopping at a light earns nothing. A line of stars is never behind slow traffic: it takes its lane only as it comes into view (the lane with the fewest cars ahead, `placeLines()`), and a car in that lane signals and moves over, or holds back until it can (`yieldToStars()`). From 50 lanes before the end the right turn signal blinks (amber lamps on the car, two arrowheads beside it, a soft tick from `sound.blink()`), so the child knows the stop is near. Then the car takes itself to the right lane, turns into a side street and stops at the door of the goal while the picture slides over to follow. The place's name shows and is said (`الْمَسْجِدُ` / `place-masjid`, `بَيْتُ جَدَّتِي` / `place-grandma`), then `الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ` (`alhamdulillah`); at the masjid the first words of the adhan play (`audio/adhan-1.mp3`, the one of three free recordings the parents kept, cut by `tools/cut-mp3.py` and credited on `المصادر`; with more files and a higher `ADHANS` they take turns); at her house the grandmother comes out and waves. Roads are data in `ROADS`. Two of them are there twice, for the parents to compare: `الْحَيُّ الْجَدِيدُ` and `الْكُورْنِيشُ الْجَدِيدُ` (`chase: true`) are the same drives seen from behind the car (`js/chase.js`), and the pair the parents do not keep is to be deleted. Stars, the steering choice and the adhan's turn are kept in `localStorage`. The tablet's rotation lock should be on while playing, or a big turn of the wheel rotates the picture.
- `js/chase.js`: the picture of the two cloned roads, on `drive.js`'s logic (the same stars, lights, crossings, pedals, steering and arrival). The father still got a little dizzy from the view from above, where the whole screen slides down at one speed with nothing standing still; a motorcycle game he sent, seen from the rider's seat, does not make him dizzy, and these roads do what it does: a view from low behind the car in simple 3D (the road drawn in slices by distance, drawings scaled the same way), a horizon 40% down that never moves, a still sky and far town, a camera that looks straight ahead and never turns or leans, the child's red car always the same size low in the middle, thin pale lane dashes that fade before they reach the car, plain curbs, and everything coming in out of a haze. He said that cured the dizziness, and sent back the rest, which is how it now stands. Every distance along the road is drawn `STRETCH` (2.5) times longer while cars, stars and buildings keep their size (and the logic's car lengths are divided by it), so that 15 lanes a second covers the ground fast: from behind, the unstretched road crawled, and 9 lanes a second was still too slow. Traffic drives at 4.5 lanes a second, mostly one car to a row and now and then two to weave between, the rows 12 to 18 lanes apart (neither the crowd he first sent back nor an empty road), and every car is drawn solid, its colors mixed toward the haze, never see-through and never left out. A car beside the child's car is a wall (`touchBeside()`: his car is put back sideways with the bump and the horn, and the other car never moves; before, it was pushed behind him and vanished); a car he has passed drops back and is gone for good once behind the camera, and one just passed, still between him and the camera, holds its lane against him for that moment. Cars keep 2 lanes behind the car ahead (`KEEP`) and stop 1 lane short of a line or a crossing (`short` in `stopLimit()`), so each shows above the roof of the one behind it. The stars of a line are 3.2 lanes apart (`STARS_APART.behind`) and stand small, facing the camera, so a line of four reads as four. A finger steers along the road as well as across it (`chase.pointed`). At the end a side street leaves the right lane at a slant, straightens, and ends at the place itself, which stands across its end facing the camera, big, a few lanes beyond the car's nose (`SLIP`, `slipOff()` in `drive.js`); the camera moves sideways with the car (`trip.pan`) while the horizon, the sky and the far town stay still; at her house the grandmother walks out to beside the car's nose and waves. A first ending at the curb, with the place 22 lanes further on, was sent back: no side road, and the grandmother small in the distance. The knobs for comfort and speed are `STRETCH`, `BACK` (the camera's distance), `HAZE`, `ROW_AT` and `POST_AT` (how far back the buildings and the lamps stand) and `DASH`, and in `drive.js` `SLIP`, `KEEP` and `STARS_APART`. On localhost, `window.driveGame.chase` and `driveGame.render()` step it by hand.
- `js/desert.js`: `الْوَاحَةُ`, the third ride in `drive.html`, a game of its own that leans on `drive.js` (its canvas, steering, pedals, stars, sounds and arrival screen). An all-terrain bike rides across the desert to an oasis (`place-oasis`), seen from behind in simple 3D: the camera rides a little above and straight behind the bike, looking straight ahead, never turning, rolling or pitching, with the horizon fixed 40% down and the sky, the sun and the far hills painted once per ride and standing still (the same cure for the father's dizziness as in `chase.js`). The bike is the Recraft drawing `atv.svg`, seen from straight behind in the middle of the picture, leaning a few degrees into a turn; its rider is a grown-up in a helmet with his back to us, so no face shows. (The father first asked for the whole bike seen from the back left; that drawing looked as if it pointed left while it went straight, and was replaced.) Two earlier views were sent back: from the side (no steering, the same course every time), then from straight above (the hills did not show, and the bike crossed water as if by magic). The ground has a height along the way (`land`) and the camera follows the ground under the bike, so a climb shows as sand rising over the horizon, a top as sand falling away, and a descent as land spread out below; the bike slows uphill and runs faster down, the motor works harder on a climb, and small puffs of dust rise low behind the wheels on slopes. There is no road: the way is shown by pairs of flags, a faint pair of old tire tracks, the lines of stars, and the oasis palms growing on the horizon near the end. The child cannot wander: the ride only goes forward, beyond the flags the sand is slow and eases the bike back, and the bike takes a share of each bend by itself (`FOLLOW`, 30%). No seat belt, so the choice goes straight to the du'a. Every ride is put together anew by `make()`: the mountain and the dunes, plus three of five other stretches (a ridge, a hollow, rocks, wells, a caravan walking beside the way), shuffled, then the palm grove and the oasis, at one of three times of day. Nothing makes the bike wait and there is no water to cross: the only water is the pool at the oasis. Rocks and wells in the way are a soft bump the bike slides around. The bike leaves the ground only off a jump ramp, which is real ground in `land` (`RAMP`: a straight face of 12 lanes up to a lip 2 lanes high, a drop behind it, two red-and-white posts at the lip; two in the dunes, one after the ridge's tall dune, none on the mountain): a bike that simply jumped on level sand was sent back. On the face the motor holds the bike at one speed (`PACE.air`), so every flight is the same true arc of about 6 lanes with three stars hung on it, and the camera follows the ground, not the bike, so the bike is seen leaving its shadow. The bike eases past a flag's pole or a ramp's post rather than through it (`POLE`); no flag stands where a flight lands, or within 3 lanes of a rock or a well. On every climb `اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ` shows and is said (`allahu-akbar`), on every descent `سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ` (`subhanallah`): a climb or a descent is a stretch of at least `ZIKR_RUN` lanes steeper than `ZIKR_SLOPE`, worked out once from `land` (`slopes()`), so the dunes, a ramp's face, the ridge, the hollow and the mountain count and the gentle swells do not. The words show only while the bike is on it and go the moment it ends; a first version that showed them on the mountain only, and kept them up after, was sent back. Animals are friends in the scenery, drawn without eyes or other features like every figure: camels with their herder, gazelles, a fox, and an eagle that glides across the sky and cries (`sound.eagle()`). The generator's first numbers are always small, so `make()` throws two away before it shuffles. On localhost, `window.driveGame.desert` steps it by hand.
- `making.html`: `الْبِنَاءُ وَالتَّصْنِيعُ`, the door on the games hub that holds the building game and the car factory.
- `stories.html`: `الْقَصَصُ`, the shelf: one book per finished story (a closed Arabic book seen face on, its spine on the right, its picture and title on the cover), no placeholders. The plan, the parents' decisions and the sources are in `docs/stories-plan.md`.
- `story.html`, `js/story.js`, `stories/<id>.json`: the story reader, `story.html?s=<id>` (the logo and ✕ lead back to the shelf). A story is data: its title, title picture and title clip, then its pages, each with its text, its picture and its clip. The first is `stories/zamzam.json`, `إِسْمَاعِيلُ الصَّغِيرُ وَبِئْرُ زَمْزَمَ`: thirteen pages in the father's own wording (2026-11-24), Claude adding only the missing vowel marks and one grammar fix he was told about (`سَبْعَةَ أَشْوَاطٍ`, as شَوْط is masculine). Its honorifics stand where he wrote them, at the first mention of each name, plus one `عَلَيْهِمَا السَّلَامُ` after Hajar and Ismail on the last page (no general rule yet: saying them every time is too much for a child, he said); edit the text only with the parents' say. Each page shows its picture above its words (beside them on a low, wide screen), large Noto Naskh, never scrolling, and nothing moves. Following the words is the parents' choice, "ink filling in": while a page is read aloud its words wait in a soft grey and each turns to full ink as it is spoken, and stays inked; the times come from the recording through `timing.autoWords` (a bare alif opening a word is passed as a wasla, as in the driving game's du'a), told where the punctuation is (`breaks`), so a pause the recording holds is matched to a comma or a full stop before anywhere else (`BREAK_COST` in `timing.js`; the Quran and the du'a pass no breaks and are timed as before). A page with no recording shows all its words inked. - `js/timing.js`: automatic word timing, ported from IQRA. Letters get tajweed weights (short vowel 1, sukoon 1.2, madd 2/4/6, shadda +0.8, ghunna +0.9, ...; silent letters such as the wasla and the sun lam get none) and the clip's speech span is split by the sums per word.
