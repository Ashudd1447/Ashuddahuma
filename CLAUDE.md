# Ashuddahuma

An Islamic homeschool site for a 4-year-old, built by his parents. The curriculum is in `Ashuddahuma Homeschool — Curriculum Outline (Age 4) V2.pdf`, one folder above this repo.

- Live site: https://ashudd.web.app
- Firebase project: `ashudd-1b0dc`, hosting site `ashudd`, web app `ashudd` (its public config is in `js/account.js`)
- GitHub: `Ashudd1447/Ashuddahuma` (public)
- Deploy: `firebase deploy --only hosting` from this folder

## Working with the parents

- Run all content past them before it goes in: photos, wording, voweling, audio. The parents give the outline and structure; content may be drafted or generated with AI tools, then a parent reviews and approves it. Nothing the child sees goes in on Claude's own initiative.
- Ask clarifying questions and consult often. Bring options with a recommendation.
- Do not commit, push, or deploy without their go-ahead.

## Content rules

- No music. Chimes for praise and a soft buzzer for errors are approved.
- Photos of people and animals may show faces. Graphics (figures, icons) must not show facial features: a plain face, with hands, clothes and other details drawn. Images may be photographs or made with AI tools (ChatGPT, Recraft) once a parent approves them. Wikimedia photos need a credit on `المصادر`; generated ones do not.
- In every picture, people eat, drink and brush their teeth with the right hand, and nobody sleeps on the tummy (on the right side, the blanket up to the shoulders and over the feet). Check generated pictures for this as for faces: image models get hands wrong often.
- Generated pictures come only from the family's paid Recraft plan (Basic, from 2026-11-19). Free-plan images are public and owned by Recraft, and upgrading does not transfer them, so a Free-plan picture never goes on the site. How to make them: `docs/recraft-images.md`.
- No churches or other non-Islamic religious buildings in photos. Check the source file name and page, not just the picture.
- Everything the child sees is in Arabic, with no English. Two exceptions: the `المصادر` page, where names and license codes may stay in Latin letters, and the admin pages, which are in English.
- Modern Standard Arabic spelling everywhere except Quran text: the circular sukoon, no dagger alif, and so on. Words the child reads are fully voweled, buttons and menu labels included (`اِسْتَمِعْ`, `أَعِدِ الْآيَةَ`, `الْآيَةُ التَّالِيَةُ`, `فُصْحَى`).
- Exceptions in the reading game: `أحسنت` is displayed with no vowel marks and recorded with a sukoon on the ت, so it is gender neutral. The prompts `اقرأ` and `اسحب كل صورة إلى كلمتها` and the title `معلم القراءة` appear exactly as the parents wrote them.
- Two dialects, one text. On-screen text is always Fusha. A spoken prompt may exist in Fusha (`audio/<name>`) and Egyptian (`audio/<name>-masri`); the dialect switch in the ☰ menu (showing `فُصْحَى` or the Egyptian flag, and saying `فصحى` / `مصري` when tapped) picks which one plays, and `sound.say(name)` falls back to Fusha when the Egyptian clip is missing. Egyptian text appears only in `record.html`, to be read aloud. Counting and every other spoken prompt follow this rule unless the parents specify one dialect.
- The installed app is named `أَشُدَّهُمَا`. The home page tab title stays `معلم القراءة`.
- Quran text is copied from a verified source, never typed by hand, and never altered. Ayah 18:82 comes from the `text_uthmani` field of the Quran.com API, with the word `أشدهما` in gold. Surah titles are the one piece of Quranic-style text typed by hand, in Uthmani spelling and the Mushaf font: `سُورَةُ ٱلْفَاتِحَةِ`.
- The Mushaf font draws the khaa-head sukoon on U+06E1 and the silent-letter circle on U+0652, while the Quran.com text writes the sukoon as U+0652; its digits already sit inside the ayah ornament, so U+06DD before a number draws a second, empty one. `mushafText()` in `app.js` swaps and drops these for display only. The stored text and the HTML keep the API's characters. Ayah numbers in the Mushaf font are the bare Arabic-Indic digits.
- Hadith and du'a text is copied from a verified source by a script, never typed, and checked by the parents: `tools/make-riding-dua.py` writes `dua/riding.json` from `docs/sources/abudawud-2602.json` (Sunan Abi Dawud 2602 as printed at sunnah.com) and, for the verses inside it, the Quran.com API. The one change the script makes is spelling: sunnah.com stores the marks of lam-alif after the alif, and they are moved onto the lam.
- Signing in is optional and says so (`اِخْتِيَارِيٌّ`); the whole site works signed out. For now an account only identifies the admin. Child profiles under a parent will build on it later; what signing up gives the family is to be written then.

## Code

Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript in `public/`, with no build step and nothing to install.

- `index.html`: the optional sign-in button (top left), logo, ayah 18:82, a card for each lesson, the `أَلْعَاب` door (an arch of colored tiles, drawn in SVG), and the install button
- `games.html`: `أَلْعَاب`, the games hub. One card per finished game, no placeholders for games still to come. Inside a game the logo leads back here, as it leads home from the hub and the lessons. The design plan for the games, with the parents' decisions, is `docs/games-plan.md`; how to make their pictures in Recraft is `docs/recraft-images.md`.
- `blocks.html`, `js/blocks.js`: `الْمُكَعَّبَاتُ`, the block puzzle. A 6×6 board and three pieces at a time (none longer than four), dragged on with large targets; on a touch screen the piece rides above the finger, and it drops on the nearest spot where it fits. A full row or column bursts in a wave, one star per line flies to the counter (top left, Arabic-Indic digits), and the praise shows and plays: `أحسنت` (`well-done`) for one line, `مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ` (`mashaallah`) for two or more or a cleared board. No losing: a piece with no room is dimmed, and when none has room the `قِطَعٌ جَدِيدَةٌ` button deals three that fit. Every deal fits the board, and often includes a piece that would finish a line. The board is kept in `localStorage`; `لَوْحَةٌ جَدِيدَةٌ` in the ☰ menu starts over. Tiles are glazed colors with a diamond inlay (`[data-tile]` in `style.css`); wide screens put the tray in a column beside the board.
- `clock.html`, `js/clock.js`: `السَّاعَةُ`, telling the time (whole hours for now, no prayer times: the prayers get their own game). One big clock whose hands the child turns; they move together as on a real clock. A touch takes the nearer hand (near the middle, the short one). Let go and the clock springs to the nearest whole hour and says it (`hour-1` to `hour-12`, in the chosen dialect); a tap on the clock says it again. The card above shows an hour and says it when tapped; when the clock settles on it, a chime, a star to the counter, `أحسنت`, and a new hour. Any other hour is just said, never buzzed. The sky behind follows the time of day (sun right to left, moon and stars at night, the town's windows lit), and the clock opens at the real time, rounded to the hour. Two round buttons pick the way to play, remembered with the stars in `localStorage`: the hours (a clock icon), or "my day" (the boy's picture), where the card shows a picture of his day (`images/day/<name>.webp`, from Recraft Basic) with its hour, in the order of the day, starting from the next one after the time now; when the clock matches, the sky turns to that time of day. The scene hours in `DAY` in `clock.js` are placeholders until the parents give the real ones.
- `drive.html`, `js/drive.js`: `قِيَادَةُ الْمَرْكَبَاتِ`, the driving games. Pick a road (`الْحَيُّ`, to the masjid; `كُورْنِيشُ النِّيلِ`, to the grandmother's house), drag the seat belt's tongue to the buckle (`اِرْبِطْ حِزَامَ الْأَمَانِ`, spoken as `belt`; the seat is empty, no figure), see the du'a of riding (`dua/riding.json`: `بِسْمِ اللَّهِ`, then the verses, and nothing else, the parents' choice; its words light from the `riding-dua` recording through `timing.autoWords`, and the go button comes when it ends, or after a moment while there is no recording), and drive. The ✕ at the top leaves a drive for the list of roads. Each drive has a light background sound made in `sound.js` (`ambience()`: wind, with birds in town and by the river). The road is drawn on a canvas straight from above and scrolls at one size: an earlier tilted view, where things grew as they came nearer, made the parents dizzy. Lengths are in lanes, the road three lanes wide. Cars are drawn in code; buildings, trees, boats and people are the Recraft drawings in `images/drive/*.svg`, turned into bitmaps once per screen size. Lamps and palms stand on the walk; everything else stands in a row beyond it, nothing in the road and nothing in front of anything else. Steering is by turning the tablet like a wheel and is absolute ("straight is straight", never set at the start): `steerAngle()` takes which way is down from `deviceorientation`, turns it by `window.orientation` (not `screen.orientation.angle` first: on iPad that counts from landscape), and when the system rotates the picture mid-turn keeps the old way up until the tablet is level the new way; 3° dead zone, full lock at 26°. iPhone and iPad show their Allow box on each launch, asked from the seat belt's release and again from the go button. With no sensor, or when a parent picks finger steering in the ☰ menu, a finger on the road steers; arrow keys always work. Two pedals, either one: held, the car speeds up to 9 lanes a second; let go, it slows. Traffic is laid out in rows well apart with a lane always open, and all of it keeps one speed, so there is always a way past; after a stop it pulls away lane by lane. No losing: the car follows traffic by itself, a touch is a soft bump with a horn, and it stops itself at a red light or for people crossing (the `stops` of a road in `ROADS`), the pedals showing a red hand meanwhile. The reward is on the road: stars in short lines to drive over, each `pop()` a little higher than the one before, and five more for arriving; stopping at a light earns nothing. At the end the car takes itself to the right lane, turns into a side street and stops at the door of the goal while the picture slides over to follow. The place's name shows and is said (`الْمَسْجِدُ` / `place-masjid`, `بَيْتُ جَدَّتِي` / `place-grandma`), then `الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ` (`alhamdulillah`); at the masjid the first words of the adhan play (`audio/adhan-1.mp3`, the one of three free recordings the parents kept, cut by `tools/cut-mp3.py` and credited on `المصادر`; with more files and a higher `ADHANS` they take turns); at her house the grandmother comes out and waves. Roads are data in `ROADS`. Stars, the steering choice and the adhan's turn are kept in `localStorage`. The tablet's rotation lock should be on while playing, or a big turn of the wheel rotates the picture.
- `js/desert.js`: `الصَّحْرَاءُ`, the third choice in `drive.html`, a game of its own that leans on `drive.js` (its canvas, pedals, stars, sounds and arrival screen). An all-terrain bike seen from the side rides to an oasis (`الْوَاحَةُ`, `place-oasis`), heading left as the bar along the top does; its rider is a grown-up in a helmet, so no face is drawn. The ground is level sand plus shapes laid out once per ride: dunes to bounce over, ramps to fly off, and mountains to climb and come down. The bike leaves the ground when the sand falls away faster than it can fall, and always lands softly. Either pedal makes it go, a climb slows it, and it never stops for good; there is no steering and no seat belt, so the choice goes straight to the du'a. Going up a mountain `اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ` shows and is said (`allahu-akbar`), coming down `سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ` (`subhanallah`). Stars hang over the dunes and along the jumps. The sun sits behind drifting clouds and shows now and then, warming the picture. The parents asked for the side view's ingredients (hills, little flights, sky); the view itself was Claude's choice.
- `reading.html`, `js/reading.js`: the reading game (picture grid, practice, matching, praise)
- `fatihah.html`, `js/recite.js`: the reciter, with two modes chosen by the tabs at the top and remembered on the device. `السُّورَةُ كَامِلَةً`: one button (`اِسْتَمِعْ` / `قِفْ`) plays the surah through. `آيَةً آيَةً`: each ayah in the list carries a play sign; a tap plays it, then the prompt `الْآنَ اقْرَأْهَا أَنْتَ` shows and is spoken (`sound.say('read-it')`), and `أَعِدِ الْآيَةَ` / `الْآيَةُ التَّالِيَةُ` move on; the last ayah has no "next" and there is no praise screen. In both modes the whole surah sits on top and the ayah being played shows big below with each word lit from the recording's clock. The page reads `quran/<surah>.json` and `audio/<surah>.timings.json`.
- `quran/<surah>.json`: the surah text, written by a script from the Quran.com API (`text_uthmani`, word by word). Never edit it by hand.
- `js/timing.js`: automatic word timing, ported from IQRA. Letters get tajweed weights (short vowel 1, sukoon 1.2, madd 2/4/6, shadda +0.8, ghunna +0.9, ...; silent letters such as the wasla and the sun lam get none) and the clip's speech span is split by the sums per word. When the recording pauses between words (a quiet stretch of at least 2 harakat at that reciter's pace, never less than 0.12 s, so a whispered sukoon letter inside a word is not taken for a pause), each voiced stretch is given the run of words whose weights best fit its length and the words share it by weight; a word before a pause stays lit until the next stretch starts. The reciter times every ayah this way as the clip decodes, and prefers a tapped timing from the file when it has the right length. The same module gives per-letter boundaries (`autoBoundaries`) for the lesson that will light letters. Codepoints follow the Quran.com text: sukoon U+0652 (U+06E1 accepted), round zero U+06DF.
- `audio/<surah>.timings.json`: only the ayat the parents fixed by tapping in `timing.html`: per ayah, the second at which each word begins plus the end of the clip (words + 1 numbers). An ayah not in the file uses the automatic timing; a wrong-length entry is ignored. The same shape, with one number per letter, will serve letter-by-letter highlighting later.
- `js/sound.js`: all sound. Recordings are fetched and decoded ahead of the tap and played through Web Audio, which is what cured the tap-to-sound lag (ported from IQRA). A name without an extension is looked for as `.m4a` then `.wav`. One clip plays at a time; a missing clip stays silent. `sound.current().position()` is the clock the highlighting runs on; `sound.buffer(name)` hands the decoded clip to the timing. The dialect setting (`sound.dialect()`, `setDialect()`, `say()`, `preloadPrompts()`) lives here too, in `localStorage`. Synthesized sounds: `chime()` for praise, `buzz()` / `wrong()` for errors, and `tick()`, a short soft tap when a block puzzle piece lands. `click()` (a seat belt), `honk()` (a car's short double horn) and `pop()` (a star picked up) are the driving games', as are `ambience()` (wind and birds, made from filtered hiss and short whistles, the parents' request, not music) and `engine()` (the desert bike's light motor). Approved for the obstacle course, not yet made: a soft jump and a pop.
- `js/app.js`: loaded by every page. `mushafText()` prepares Quran text for the Mushaf font (see Content rules). Keeps the screen on and lets it sleep after 5 minutes without a touch (installed iPhone/iPad apps need iOS 18.4). Registers the offline copy. Shows the `تَثْبِيتُ التَّطْبِيقِ` button on the home page when the browser offers installing: Android, and Chrome or Edge on computers, never iPhone or iPad. Builds the ☰ menu at the top right of every Arabic page (the dialect switch), and beside it, on every page but the home page, the ✕ that leaves a game for the games hub and anything else for the home page.
- `js/account.js`: accounts, as in IQRA: Firebase Authentication over its REST API (email and password, no SDK), the session in `localStorage`, Arabic error messages. On the home page it builds the sign-in button and panel. The admin is the account whose email's SHA-256 is `ADMIN_EMAIL_HASH`; the email itself is never written in this public repo. Signed in as the admin, the panel shows the `صَفْحَةُ الْمُشْرِفِ` link; nobody else sees any sign of the admin pages. The Email/Password provider must be enabled in the Firebase console (Authentication → Sign-in method) for sign-in to work.
- Admin pages, English, not linked from the site: `admin.html` (also served at `/admin` by a hosting rewrite in `firebase.json`), `js/admin.js` (the guard: `account.verifyAdmin()` asks the auth server whose token this is, so a session typed into `localStorage` opens nothing; on localhost, the admin's own computer, the pages open freely; anyone else is sent to the home page), `css/admin.css`. Nothing on the server is behind this: the tools only write files on the admin's computer. These pages are not precached by `sw.js`.
- `record.html`, `js/record.js`: the recording tool. One card per clip the site plays (Egyptian cards next to their Fusha ones), raw microphone capture (no echo cancellation, noise suppression or auto gain), silence trimmed, written as 16-bit mono WAV at 22,050 Hz into `public/audio` through the folder picker (Chrome or Edge on a computer) or downloaded. Add a card for every new clip. The page opens showing only the clips still to record, with a count, so everything can be recorded in one sitting. This is where every recording for the site and the games is made: `/admin` → Record audio, on a computer.
- `timing.html`: the word-timing check. Preview plays an ayah with the automatic timing (or the tapped one when the file has it); Play and tap records a tapped timing for an ayah whose automatic one is off; download the file.
- `strokes.html`, `js/strokes.js`, `js/tracing.js`: the stroke editor for the tracing lesson. The admin draws each letter's strokes, in order and direction, on the Noto Naskh glyph (a drag is a stroke, a tap is a dot; the hamza is drawn, not tapped) and writes `tracing/strokes.json`: strokes in board units (0 to 1 of the square) plus the glyph's ink box. `tracing.js` holds the layout rule (ink box fills at most 0.72 of the board, centered) that the lesson must share so the strokes land on the same glyph.
- `sw.js`: the offline copy. Add every new page, photo, recording, or font to `FILES`. Saved files open first and update in the background, so a deploy shows up on the second launch. On localhost the newest files come first.
- `manifest.webmanifest`, `icons/`: the installed app's name, colors, and icons, made from `images/logo.webp`. iPhone and iPad cache icons hard, so a replaced icon needs a new file name.
- `credits.html`: `المصادر`, the required photo credits. Update it whenever a photo changes.
- `audio/<name>.m4a` or `.wav`: the parents' own recordings (`.m4a` from a phone, `.wav` from `record.html`). The games play them by name, and a missing clip stays silent. Add every new clip to `FILES` in `sw.js`. Clip names so far: the six words, `read`, `match`, `well-done`; `fatihah-1` to `fatihah-7`; `read-it` / `read-it-masri`; `fusha` / `masri`; `jump` / `jump-masri`, `count-1` to `count-7` (+ `-masri`); `father`, `mother`, `brother`, `alif-name`, `alif-sound`; `mashaallah`; `hour-1` to `hour-12` (+ `-masri`); `masjid-dua`; `belt` / `belt-masri`, `riding-dua`, `alhamdulillah`, `place-masjid` / `place-grandma` / `place-oasis` (+ `-masri`), `allahu-akbar`, `subhanallah`. Only the first nine exist; the rest are cards waiting to be recorded.
- `images/day/*.webp`, `images/drive/*.svg`: the Recraft pictures. The driving game's are vector drawings cut out and slimmed by `clean-svg.py`, kept with the raw files in the `recraft` folder one level above this repo (see `docs/recraft-images.md`).
- `audio/adhan-1.mp3`: the opening words of a freely licensed adhan recording (the KLCC masjid in Kuala Lumpur, CC0), the only recording on the site that is not the parents' own. The full files are in `adhan-raw`, one level above this repo. No free recording of the muezzins the parents asked for (Ali Mulla, al-Surayhi, the Egyptian radio adhan) was found: those belong to their broadcasters.
- `tools/`: one-off Python scripts that write data files from their sources (standard library only). The site itself still needs no build step.
- `images/<name>.jpg`: Wikimedia Commons photos, cropped to square by CSS. To be replaced by approved generated images; the Kaaba and the Prophet's mosque are the ones most likely to stay real.
- `fonts/`: KFGQPC Uthmanic Script HAFS (`UthmanicHafs1-Ver09.otf`) for Quran text, the `.quran` class. Its license allows copying and serving the original file but forbids changing it: never subset it or convert it to WOFF2, and keep `KFGQPC-LICENSE.txt` beside it. Noto Naskh Arabic for everything else (the parents' choice), Scheherazade New as the Quran fallback (both SIL Open Font License).

Pages must work at phone, tablet, and computer sizes, right to left. Wrong matches vibrate on touch devices that support it (not iPhone or iPad) and otherwise play the buzzer.

The parents work on Windows and Mac. `.gitattributes` keeps LF line endings everywhere. Keep file names lowercase: both computers ignore case, but the live server does not.

The IQRA app (`C:\Claude Projects\Arabic App\1447 H` on Kareem's PC, React + TypeScript) is the source of the sound, highlighting, timing, recording, accounts and tracing designs. Port ideas and data shapes from it; never copy its React code.
